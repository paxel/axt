Records go to the app of another user only through a share: a rule in the sending app that names, for one receiver, the records that go there. When a record leaves the share, the app takes it back from that receiver with a withdrawal.

Only predefined records leave

A share names single records, or the records that a rule of the app chooses, such as every Appointment of one pet (shares, section 1). An app sends an app of another user only the records shared with it: every first message, update, deletion and resend, in a live connection, through a relay and in a file (section 2.2).

  • A user who wants to send one record to another user puts it into a share for that user's app; a share of a single record allows that. The app sends such a receiver a record in no other way, also not one its user picks at an offer.
  • An export, a file for no particular receiver, holds only the records its user chose for it. That choice is its share.
  • Messages that carry no record, such as the manifest, offers and acknowledgements, leave as before.

Nothing in the protocol can check this: a receiver sees records, not the rules that chose them. The rule binds the sending app.

Which apps are another user's

The protocol knows apps by their keys, not their users. An app learns from its own user which of the apps it exchanges with belong to another user, for example when the user accepts a key at a first contact (section 2.3). An app may send records to an app of its own user, such as another app on the same phone, without a share, with its user's answer at each offer. An app may treat every receiver as another user's, and one that cannot tell must. The owner's own devices are no receivers: they get every record by sync.

Where the shares live

The specification does not say how a share looks, how a user makes one, or how a rule picks records (section 2.1). It does say where they live:

  • The app keeps its shares as records of a type in its own namespace, records of its own set like any other. Sync carries every type, so every device of the owner holds the same shares (Run on several devices).
  • In the same records, the app keeps each withdrawal it made: the record's id, the receiver and the withdrawal's version. Its devices need these to send a withdrawal again and to share a record again.
  • The app may keep these in one type or in several, under names of its choice.

The receiver sees no share. It gets offers and records as from any sender.

Withdrawing a record

A record leaves the shares for a receiver when its user takes it out of a share, changes or ends a rule so that it no longer names the record, or changes the record so that a rule no longer picks it, such as by removing the tag the rule chose it by (section 6.1). The app then withdraws it from that receiver: it sends a WITHDRAW, a record message under a version of its own that names the receiver's device (section 3).

  • The device on which the change is made sends the withdrawal. A sibling that learns of the change by sync sends the same recorded withdrawal, not one of its own.
  • Ending a share withdraws every record it named that no other share for that receiver names, one withdrawal per record (section 6.4). The two apps stay known to each other.
  • A record whose last message to the receiver was a deletion need not be withdrawn.
  • Putting the record into a share again sends it under a new, higher version, also when it has not changed. The receiver takes it anew under the same id, or merges it into a copy it kept (section 6.2).

What the receiver does

A receiver handles a withdrawal as it handles a deletion (section 4): it removes its copy and keeps a tombstone, and shows the record as it shows one its sender deleted. It may tell its user that the sender no longer shares it.

When the receiver's user has changed the record, the receiving app decides (section 4.3):

  • Remove it. The user's changes go with the record. A record shared again later starts without them.
  • Keep it with the user's changes. It stays a record of its sender under the same id; when it is shared again, the next update is merged into it. The sender can take it no further.
  • Ask the user. Until the user decides, the receiver keeps it and reports so, and reports again if the user gives it up.

A record that its user has not changed is always removed. The choice for a withdrawal may differ from the choice for a deletion; the devices of one owner make it alike. The outcome is WITHDRAWN, or KEPT for a kept record (section 5.1), and the sending app may show its user which it was.

What a withdrawal cannot do

A withdrawal has limits a designer should know (section 9):

  • It is a request. It does not reach what the other user saw, copied into a record of their own or passed on outside AXT, and an app that does not conform can keep every record it got.
  • It is bound to its receiver. A third app that gets it passed on ignores it.
  • It can tell ids. A withdrawal of a record that never reached the receiver tells the receiver its id, and the withdrawals at the end of a share tell it how many records the share named.
  • It needs an accepted type. A receiver that no longer takes the record's type gets no withdrawal until it takes the type again, and keeps the record meanwhile.
