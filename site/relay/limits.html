Some limits are the same for every relay: the size of a message and the holding time transports.holding. Others are the reference relay's own choice, and most of them are options. This page names both, and what the relay keeps on disk.

Size and room

From relay.md, section 4.1:

LimitValue
A message1 byte to transports.sealed_message, the same for every relay; a larger Content-Length gets 413 (transports.md, section 5.6)
The room of a mailbox64 MiB of held messages by default (--quota-bytes); a put that would go above it gets 507, except into an empty mailbox, which always takes one message
The number of messagesNo limit beyond the room; a list shows the oldest transports.list_messages
Who may create a mailboxEvery client, within the rate limits

The room counts the bytes of the messages held, without the header of a message file. A message past its holding time does not count. At most 1,000 lists wait at the same time (--max-waiting); a further list answers at once (relay.md, section 3.3).

Times

From relay.md, section 4.2. None of them is an option:

TimeValue
Holding timetransports.holding from the put, the same for every relay (transports.md, section 5.5). From then on a message is neither listed nor fetched nor counted
Deleting itAt the next sweep, at most 10 minutes later, or earlier when a put, list or fetch touches its mailbox
A repeated putChanges nothing; it does not extend the holding time
An idle mailboxRemoved with its messages at the first sweep at least one day more than transports.mailbox_idle after the stored time of its owner's last request. That time is written at most once a day, so a mailbox is never removed before transports.mailbox_idle (transports.md, section 5.5)
The sweepEvery 10 minutes

Rate limits

Each client address has two token buckets, which refill evenly over their period (relay.md, section 4.3):

LimitDefaultOption
Requests per minute, of every kind600--rate-requests
Mailboxes created per hour, only new ones20--rate-creations

A request above a limit gets 429 with Retry-After, the whole seconds until the bucket holds a token again. 0 turns a limit off. There is no limit per mailbox beyond its room: whoever knows a mailbox id can put messages into it, and the room bounds what they can put. Behind a proxy, name the client address header, or every client shares the proxy's buckets (Put a proxy in front).

What is on disk

The storage directory, --storage, holds (relay.md, section 5.1):

PathContent
mailboxes/<mailbox id>/One directory per mailbox, named by its id
mailboxes/<mailbox id>/<message id>One file per message held, named by its SHA-256
mailboxes/<mailbox id>/seenThe time of the owner's last request, in milliseconds, as decimal text
tmp/Files being written, and mailboxes being removed; emptied at each start

A message file is a header of 20 bytes, with the time of the put and a sequence number, followed by the sealed message as it was put (relay.md, section 5.2). The relay writes seen when a mailbox is created and then at most once a day. It keeps no mailbox secret: it checks a secret by computing the mailbox id from it.

A put is first written under tmp/, flushed to the disk and then renamed into its mailbox, so a crash or a full disk leaves either the whole message or none (relay.md, section 5.3). The rename is atomic within one file system. When the storage fails, such as when the disk is full, the relay answers 503 with Retry-After: 60.

Copies and backups

A copy of the storage gives away the mailbox ids, the sealed messages, their sizes, and the times of their puts and of the owners' last requests. It gives away no secret and no content. A relay keeps no message beyond the holding time, also not in a backup (relay.md, section 5.4).

The storage needs no backup. A lost storage loses messages, which their senders send again after the holding time (protocol.md, section 11), and owners create their mailboxes again after a 404 (transports.md, section 5.4).
