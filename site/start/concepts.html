Seven ideas carry most of AXT. Each section below says what one of them is, in a few sentences, and links the section of the specification that defines it. The Glossary holds every term.

Record

A record is one datum of a type, such as one Appointment, together with its id and its version. The id is a UUID that the sender gives the record when it creates it, and the record keeps it for its whole life. The version rises with every change; a receiver applies a message only if its version is higher than the one it holds. A deletion is a message too, and leaves a tombstone at the receiver, so that an older message cannot bring the record back.

Spec: Records, sections 1 to 5. Glossary: record, version, tombstone.

Sender

The sender of a record is the app that created it and sends its versions. Only the sender sends versions of a record. A receiver knows a sender by the first key it accepted from it and by the keys added to it since, and applies an update or a deletion only from that sender. The receiver's user may change a received record in the receiver; that change stays there and is never sent back as a version.

Spec: Records, section 1; Keys, section 3. Glossary: sender, receiver, local change.

Device set

An owner who runs an app on a phone and a tablet has two installations, each with a signing key of its own. Together they are one device set, and other apps take the set for one sender. Any device of the set can add a new device, by QR code, and remove another, such as a lost one. Every device changes every record of the set: each change carries a stamp from a clock that never runs backwards, and per field the later change wins. After a record's first transfer, the devices send each other only its changed fields. Another app has to reach only one device of the set, which passes the message on to the others.

Spec: Keys, section 5; Sync, sections 2 to 4 and section 17. Glossary: the device set is part of sender; see also sibling and stamp.

Manifest

The manifest is the message in which an app names the types it takes, in which versions, and, in one sentence per type, what it does with them: "An Appointment becomes a Reminder." An app may also take any type. A sender offers only types that fit the receiver's manifest, and shows its user the receiver's sentence before the user chooses what to send. The app name in a manifest is what the app says about itself; the key identifies it.

Spec: Manifest, section 3 and section 5; schema axt.Manifest. Glossary: manifest.

Offer

An offer names the types whose records the sender wants to send, with their full writer schemas. The receiver answers each type with an accept or a decline; a type that fits the manifest is still only taken when the receiver accepts it. After that, each record in a live connection names its schema by a fingerprint only. A file has no offer: its header carries the schemas instead.

Spec: Protocol, section 4 and section 5; schema axt.Offer. Glossary: offer, fingerprint.

Outcome

The outcome is what a receiver did with a record message, and it reports it in an acknowledgement:

OutcomeMeaning
APPLIEDThe receiver took the record, or deleted it, as the message says.
MERGEDIt took the record except the fields its user had changed.
KEPTIt took nothing, because its user's change covers the whole record.
DUPLICATEIt already had this version.
STALEIt already had a higher version.
WITHDRAWNIt removed its copy of a record that its sender withdrew from it, or held none.

A reader of a file reaches the same outcomes but reports nothing. Spec: Records, section 7; the outcomes on the Errors page. Glossary: outcome.

Share

To an app of another user, only what the owner chose beforehand leaves. A share is a rule inside the sending app that names, for one such app, the records that go to it: single records, or the records a rule of the app picks. The receiver sees no share, only ordinary records. The owner can withdraw a record from a share; the other app then removes its copy, or may keep it if its user changed it. Where the owner's app takes proposals for a type, the other user can propose a change of a shared record, which the owner's user takes or refuses; a change taken goes out as an ordinary update.

Spec: Shares, section 2, section 3 and section 12. Glossary: share, withdrawal, proposal.
